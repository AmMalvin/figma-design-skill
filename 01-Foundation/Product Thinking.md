# Product thinking

Owns problem/outcome framing. Research methods live in [Design Research](Design%20Research.md); dependencies live in [Systems Thinking](Systems%20Thinking.md).

Identify user segment/context, task, current friction, outcome, constraints and evidence. Distinguish a feature request from a validated problem. Respect supplied requirements; challenge harmful decisions with evidence without silently changing scope.

- Choose task outcomes and guardrails. Completion, accuracy and confidence may matter more than engagement; more clicks/sessions are not inherently better.
- Treat metrics as hypotheses until observed. "Reduce failed transfers" needs a baseline and a distinction between UX and service failures.
- Compare reach, severity, frequency, reversibility and engineering effort. Use prioritization scores only with meaningful inputs; guessed precision hides uncertainty.
- Scope the smallest coherent flow, including costly failure paths. One happy-path screen is insufficient.
- Separate interface behavior from business policy. Do not invent fees, retention, fraud rules or privileges.
- Preserve adoption and migration: an expert shortcut must not conceal an essential action from occasional users.

Deliver facts, assumptions, constraints, outcome and next verification. [Jobs To Be Done](Jobs%20To%20Be%20Done.md) identifies progress; [Design Decision Engine](../00-Core/Design%20Decision%20Engine.md) owns selection.

Example: a billing administrator needs reconciliation. Prioritize exceptions, ownership and next actions; a revenue tile earns space only if it changes that decision.
