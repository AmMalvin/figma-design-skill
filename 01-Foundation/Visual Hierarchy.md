# Visual hierarchy

Owns attention, grouping and reading order. Begin with information priority before containers.

Identify what users notice, compare, decide and act on. Use type, contrast, proximity, alignment and space to communicate relationships. One decision can have one emphasis; expert monitoring can require concurrent priorities.

- Group by meaning; spacing/type/dividers often beat another card.
- Align comparable values rather than scattering metrics.
- Reserve strongest emphasis for current task or critical exception; equal tiles imply equal importance.
- Establish readable regions and an action path; do not impose F/Z scanning diagrams universally.
- Balance density against context. Whitespace earns its space through comprehension, pacing or input comfort.
- Keep hierarchy understandable without color or motion; secondary metadata remains readable.

Test real content, long labels, missing values, more records and intermediate widths. A sparse attractive frame is not proof of comprehension.
