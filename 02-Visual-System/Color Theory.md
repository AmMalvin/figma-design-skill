# Color decisions

Owns visual color usage; [Token Architecture](../03-Design-Tokens/Token%20Architecture.md) owns representation and aliases.

Inspect existing semantic roles, brand and supported modes before selecting colors. Use neutral/expressive balance appropriate to the product. Color can establish character as well as status, but must preserve hierarchy, readable content and meaningful states.

Keep roles distinct: content, surface, action, border, focus, status and categorical data. Do not call a blue ramp "semantic" or merge two purposes just because values match. Avoid using brand accent for every surface and status.

Validate contrast on actual backgrounds, including pressed/focus/selected states and overlays. Theme changes need role-by-role review rather than inversion. Do not communicate status or chart series by color alone. [Accessibility](../01-Foundation/Accessibility.md)

For data, choose sequential/diverging/categorical encoding from the measured relationship, provide labels and preserve ordering. A complementary palette is not automatically appropriate for a dashboard.

When no system exists, document a small provisional palette, roles, contrast results and mode scope. Do not invent a complete multi-brand ramp unnecessarily.

Check action visibility, status distinction, readable secondary text and cross-mode meaning. Refined color depends on controlled emphasis and context, not gratuitous gradients or saturation.
